Abstract 

We give an (1 .3865+ ε)-approximation for correlation clustering in complete graphs, improving the previous best factor of 1.485 + ε of Cao et al. (STOC’24). Our two key contributions are independent: an efficient approximate separation oracle for the cluster-LP dual and a new rounding scheme. The dual separation task is the CC-MinRatio problem: for signed vertex weights q, min-imize cost (S)/q (S) over sets with q(S) > 0; here cost (S) measures the correlation clustering disagreements attributed to S in any clustering in which S is a cluster. We give a randomized 

(1 + ε)-approximation in time 2poly(1 /ε )(n + ⟨q⟩)O(1) . Via the ellipsoid method, this yields a 

(1 + ε)-approximation of the fractional cluster-LP optimum, along with exactly feasible primal and dual solutions certifying its value. The algorithm works directly on the original instance, without a global preclustering: a new localization technique restricts the search to a small universe while preserving a violation, after which weak regularity handles the resulting dense quadratic minimization. Our rounding scheme retains the cluster-based procedure of Cao et al. but uses a continuous conditional pivot rule whose analysis rests on a single variance inequality with explicit weights and an exact computer-assisted verification of the resulting polynomial inequalities. The rounding alone bounds the cluster-LP integrality gap by 1.3865 , near its known lower bound of 4/3.Combined with the primal solver of Cao et al. (STOC’25), it gives a (1 .3865 + ε)-approximation in expected time ˜O(2 poly(1 /ε )n) in the adjacency-list query model; combined instead with our dual solver, it gives the same ratio measured against the fractional optimum in time 

˜O(2 poly(1 /ε ) poly( n)) , as well as a per-instance certificate.

Correlation Clustering is perhaps the most natural formulation of clustering, given general pairwise similarity information between objects. On a complete signed graph, with positive (resp., negative) edges representing similar (resp., dissimilar) pairs, it asks for a partition of the vertices that minimizes the number of disagreements ( MinDisagree objective): negative pairs placed in the same cluster or positive pairs placed in different clusters. We encode the positive pairs by an unweighted graph 

G = ( V, E +) and write E− = (V

\ E+ for the complementary set of negative pairs. The computational complexity of the problem and its applications to machine learning have been thoroughly investigated; see [ 11 , 13 ] and the references therein. Its contemporary form was introduced by Bansal, Blum, and Chawla [7] , albeit earlier formulations date at least as far back as the 1960s [ 38 , 41 ]. The problem is NP-hard [ 6, 33 , 40 ], motivating the search for approximation algorithms. The first polynomial-time constant-factor guarantee was given by Bansal, Blum, and Chawla [7] ; Charikar, Guruswami, and Wirth [17] later obtained a 4-approximation. Ailon, Charikar, and Newman [2] achieved ratio 3 via a simple combinatorial algorithm, and gave an LP-based refinement with ratio 2.5. Subsequently, Chawla, Makarychev, Schramm, and Yaroslavtsev [19] achieved a 2.06 -approximation, close to the factor-2 integrality gap of the standard LP relaxation [ 17 ]. Very recently, a breakthrough of Cohen-Addad, Lee, and Newman [22] broke this apparent barrier, giving an (1 .994 + ε)-approximation via the Sherali-Adams hierarchy. This spurred a sequence of improvements: Cohen-Addad, Lee, Li, and Newman [23] reduced the ratio to 1.73 + ε

and introduced the concept of preclustering, rooted in the technique of Cohen-Addad et al. [21] .Later Cao, Cohen-Addad, Lee, Li, Newman, and Vogl [14] introduced the cluster LP , an exponential-size relaxation with one variable for every possible cluster. Remarkably, the latter work succeeded in solving the cluster LP up to a constant factor, from which they derived the state-of-the-art 

(1 .485 + ε)-approximation algorithm for MinDisagree (see also [ 16 ]). Subsequent work gave, for fixed ε, ˜O(n)-time algorithms: a combinatorial (1 .847 + ε)-approximation [ 24 ], and a cluster-LP implementation achieving (1 .485 + ε) [15 ]; these algorithms are sublinear in the size of the signed graph, Θ( n2). The proven guarantees of both cluster-LP solvers [ 14 , 15 ] compare the value of the computed primal solution with the integral (not fractional) correlation-clustering optimum. 

1.1 Our contributions 

Our first result is an efficient weak separation algorithm for the cluster-LP dual (Theorem 2.1). It either finds a violated constraint or certifies feasibility after scaling the dual vector by 1 + ε.This makes the cluster LP algorithmically accessible as a relaxation in its own right, so we can approximate the fractional cluster-LP optimum, produce a feasible dual solution certifying this approximation, and recover a sparse feasible primal solution. This differs from the proven guarantees of Cao et al. [ 14 , 15 ], whose algorithms first precluster the instance and construct a feasible primal 

solution of value at most (1 + ε) times the integral optimum. They remark that their primal analysis should extend to the fractional optimum, but do not provide this extension; separately, they state that they do not know how to solve the cluster LP by considering its dual [ 16 , Section 1.1 and footnote 1]. Our result supplies an explicit self-contained dual-based proof. 

Theorem 1.1 (Approximate dual separation) . For every ε > 0, there is a randomized weak separation algorithm for the cluster-LP dual that, with probability at least 2/3, either returns a violated constraint or correctly reports that the given dual vector becomes feasible after division by 

1 + ε. Its running time is 2poly(1 /ε )(n + ⟨q⟩)O(1) . Thus, in time 2poly(1 /ε )nO(1) one can approximate the fractional cluster-LP optimum within a factor 1 + ε and produce an exactly feasible dual solution together with a feasible primal solution of polynomial support certifying this approximation. 

1Our second result is a rounding algorithm for every feasible cluster-LP solution. 

Theorem 1.2 (Rounding the cluster LP) . There is a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that, given any feasible cluster-LP solution z, returns a clustering A whose expected cost is at most 1.3865 

times the cluster LP value of z.

This result proves that the integrality gap of the cluster LP is at most 1.3865 ; combined with the known lower bound of 4/3 [14], it places the gap in the narrow interval [4 /3, 1.3865] .

Corollary 1.3. For every ε > 0, there is a (1 .3865+ ε)-factor approximation algorithm for correlation clustering in time 2poly(1 /ε ) poly( n).

In fact, the rounding theorem can be combined with either cluster-LP solver, with different benefits. Together with our first result, run with a suitably smaller accuracy parameter, it gives a computable instance-dependent certificate: the ratio between the cost of the clustering returned and the value of the feasible dual solution. Conditional on a successful LP computation, the expected value of this ratio is at most 1.3865 + ε. Alternatively, the primal solution produced by Cao et al. [ 15 ] can be fed to our rounding algorithm, giving a (1 .3865 + ε)-approximation against the integral optimum in expected time ˜O(2 poly(1 /ε )n) with adjacency-list queries.

1.3 Further related work 

The aforementioned work of Cao et al. [14] showed NP-hardness of approximation for MinDisagree 

up to a factor of 24 /23 . The weighted version, in which pairs carry arbitrary nonnegative weights, is equivalent to minimum multicut and is O(log n)-factor approximable, as observed by Charikar, Guruswami, and Wirth [17] and Demaine, Emanuel, Fiat, and Immorlica [25] . Assuming the Unique Games Conjecture, no polynomial-time O(1) -factor approximation algorithm exists in this case [ 18 ]. Ailon et al. [3] gave a query-efficient algorithm where pairwise similarities can be consulted; their running time is exponential in n. A sublinear-time approximation algorithm with a nearly optimal mixed multiplicative-additive guarantee was designed by Bonchi et al. [9] and simplified in [ 27 ]; see also [ 5 , 34 ]. The work of [ 15 , 24 ] gave sublinear-time algorithms with multiplicative guarantees, assuming the stronger adjacency-list oracle. Many other variants have been investigated, including fixed numbers of clusters [ 28 ], maximiz-ing disagreements [ 17 , 39 ], constrained cluster sizes [ 37 ], overlapping clusters [ 10 ], edge-labeled graphs [12], multilayer networks [36], hypergraphs [32, 35], and streaming settings [1, 20]. # 7 Rounding the cluster LP 

We describe how to round any feasible solution z = ( zS )S⊆V of the cluster LP. Our analysis is broadly based on the framework of Cao et al. [ 16 , Sections 2.2 and 6]. In both approaches, one runs cluster-based rounding and a pivot-based rounding (the details of which differ), analyzes the cheaper output through a convex combination, and studies the pivot procedure by assigning budgets to pairs that are released when an endpoint is removed. Cao et al.’s 1.485 analysis uses a factor-revealing SDP whose variables describe the global frequencies of discretized triangle types and whose positive-semidefinite constraints encode covariance and frequency consistency. Our rounding rule is more flexible: rather than switching among a few fixed regimes, the conditional rule (40) lets both the marginal joining probability and the loading on membership in the sampled cluster vary continuously with the LP value. The analyses also differ in how they use the global constraints. The factor-revealing reduction of Cao et al. replaces the surplus of every triangle in a discretization cell by a common lower bound before imposing the global constraints. Instead, we give an explicit certificate made from a single variance computation, with all of the rounding data and the certificate weight given in closed form, and verify the resulting local inequalities directly over the continuous locally feasible region by exact rational arithmetic.
