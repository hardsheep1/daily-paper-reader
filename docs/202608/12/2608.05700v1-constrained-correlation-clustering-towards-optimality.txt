# Constrained Correlation Clustering: Towards Optimality 

## Sina Azizeddin ∗ Evangelos Kipouridis † Nithin Varma ‡

Abstract 

In the Correlation Clustering problem, we are given an undirected graph and are tasked with com-puting a clustering (partition of the nodes) that minimizes the number of violated pairs (edges across different clusters plus non-edges within clusters). In the constrained version of this problem, the goal is to compute a clustering that satisfies additional hard constraints mandating certain pairs to be in the same cluster and certain pairs to be in different clusters. In this work, we identify Constrained Correlation Clustering as a variant of Correlation Clustering for which optimal approximations might be within reach, and make progress towards this front. Constrained Correlation Clustering is APX-Hard, and the optimal approximation factor is known to lie in ( 24 23 , 3]. We significantly tighten this gap, by showing that the optimal approximation factor lies in [2 , 16 7 − γ) for a small constant γ > 0. Our lower bound of 2 shows a separation between Correlation Clustering (which admits an 1 .485 + ϵ approximation) and Constrained Correlation Clustering 1. Our upper bound of 16 7 − γ uses the Sherali-Adams relaxation and goes beyond straightforward Triangle-Based analysis; more precisely, our algorithm belongs to a natural class of pivoting algorithms for which we prove that a straightforward Triangle-Based analysis cannot prove a better-than- 16 7 approximation. Finally, as a byproduct of our techniques, we completely resolve the approximability of Cluster Dele-tion. Cluster Deletion is a well-studied special case of Constrained Correlation Clustering for which a 2-approximation algorithm is known. We show that this is optimal, as our lower bound holds even for this special case.

Clustering is a central problem in unsupervised learning, with many applications to machine learning and data mining. The high-level goal is to partition a set of elements into clusters so that similar elements fall into the same cluster and dissimilar elements fall into different clusters. Among the various formalizations of clustering, one particularly elegant and natural formulation is that of Correlation Clustering, proposed by Bansal, Blum, and Chawla [BBC04]. Correlation Clustering captures pairwise similarities and dissimilarities among a set V of elements using a complete unweighted undirected labeled graph ( V, E + ⊎ E−), where the plus edges in E+ represent similar pairs and the minus edges in E− represent dissimilar pairs. The objective is to determine a clustering that minimizes the total number of plus edges whose endpoints lie in different clusters and minus edges whose endpoints lie in the same cluster. Correlation Clustering stands out among other clustering formulations because it does not require specifying the number of clusters as part of the input. Since its introduction, the problem has been widely studied and successfully applied to automated labeling [AHK +09, CKP08], clustering ensembles [BGU13], community detection [CSX12, VGW18], disambiguation tasks [KCMN08], duplicate detection [ARS09], and image segmentation [KNKY11, YIF12], among others. Real-world clustering tasks often come with prior knowledge that certain nodes must always be grouped together, while others must be kept apart. Several constrained clustering formulations have been pro-posed to model such requirements, including constrained k-means [WCR +01], spectral clustering with con-straints [WD10], constrained ranking and clustering [vZW09], and constrained fuzzy clustering [Pha02]. Constrained clustering is particularly valuable as it improves the quality of the resulting clusters, aligns outcomes with domain-specific knowledge, and reduces sensitivity to noisy data. In this paper, we study Constrained Correlation Clustering , a variant of Correlation Clustering introduced by van Zuylen and Williamson [vZW09] that incorporates the notion of critical pairs of nodes. The input to Constrained Correlation Clustering consists of the labeled graph ( V, E + ⊎ E−) together with a collection of hard pairwise constraints: a must-link constraint requires two nodes to be in the same cluster, while a cannot-link constraint requires them to be in different clusters. Only clusterings that satisfy all hard constraints are considered valid, and the goal is to find an optimal valid clustering. Practical applications of Constrained Correlation Clustering include, for example, clustering news articles about the same event across different languages [GZ07], where the hard constraints ensure that articles covering different events within the same language do not end up in the same cluster. There are also applications in DNA clone classification [BVDM10]. In fact, in these cases there are no must-link constraints, and all minus edges are cannot-link constraints (also known as the Cluster Deletion problem). 

## 1.1 Previous Results 

Bansal, Blum, and Chawla [BBC04], who defined Correlation Clustering, also proved that it is NP-Hard. They further provided a deterministic constant-factor approximation, where the constant is larger than 15,000. The approximation guarantee was subsequently improved further, by relying on LP-rounding tech-niques. Charikar, Guruswami and Wirth [CGW05] gave a deterministic 4-approximation, while also show-ing that the problem is APX-Hard. Ailon, Charikar and Newman [ACN08] presented a randomized 2 .5-approximation, and Chawla, Makarychev, Schramm and Yaroslavtsev [CMSY15] gave a deterministic 2 .06-approximation. The last result is nearly optimal among algorithms that round the natural LP formulation, since its integrality gap is at least 2. A breakthrough result by Cohen-Addad, Lee and Newman [CLN22] used the Sherali-Adams relaxation to obtain a (1 .994 + ϵ)-approximation and broke the 2 barrier. The ap-proximation guarantee was later improved to 1 .73 + ϵ by Cohen-Addad, Lee, Li and Newman [CLLN23], and even to 1 .485 + ϵ by [CCL +24]. The current-state of the art is an adaptation of the 1 .485 + ϵ approx-imation that runs in linear [CCL +25], and even sublinear [CCL +25] time. There is also a combinatorial 1.847-approximation by [CLP +24] for the problem. Correlation Clustering has also been studied in different settings such as dynamic algorithms [CLMP24], sublinear and streaming algorithms [AW22, CKL +24, BCMT22, BCMT23, MC23, CKL +24], parameterized 1algorithms [FKP +14], massively parallel computation (MPC) algorithms [CLM +21, CHS24], and differen-tially private algorithms [BEK21]. The approximation of the Cluster Deletion problem (special case of Constrained Correlation Clustering where there are no must-link constraints, and all minus edges are cannot-link constraints) has been extensively studied [CGW05, vZW09, BVDM10, PM15, VGW18, Vel22, BXCV24, GHR25]. The current best is a 2 approximation, by Veldt, Gleich, and Wirth [VGW18]. Subsequent research focused on faster algorithms even at the expense of worse approximation factors [Vel22, BXCV24], and better-than-2 approximations for particular graph classes [GHR25]. For Constrained Correlation Clustering the state-of-the-art is a deterministic 3-approximation, presented by van Zuylen and Williamson [vZW09], who introduced the problem. Due to the significance of the problem, a follow-up work by Fischer, Klausen, Kipouridis and Thorup [FKKT24] focused on faster algorithms for Constrained Correlation Clustering, even at the expense of the approximation guarantee (16); this was improved by Veldt to a 3+ ε approximation [Vel26]. Finally, in [KKV25] the authors show how an adaptation of the Linear Program (Cluster LP) solved in [CCL +24] would imply a better-than-2 approximation for Constrained Correlation Clustering. As a final remark, we point out that it is not possible, in general, to handle the must-link constraints by simply merging the nodes in connected components induced by these constraints into single nodes, as this results in a weighted instance outside the problem. 

## 1.2 Our Contribution and Techniques 

The optimal approximation factor for Constrained Correlation Clustering is known to lie in ( 24 23 , 3] [CCL +24, vZW09]. In this work, we tighten the gap to [2 , 16 7 − γ) for some constant γ > 0, hinting that 2 might be the optimal approximation for Constrained Correlation Clustering 2.

Lower Bound Our lower bound of 2 shows a separation between Correlation Clustering (which admits an 1.485 + ϵ approximation [CCL +24]) and Constrained Correlation Clustering. It is conditioned on the Unique Games [Kho02]. Furthermore, combined with the work of [KKV25], our work implies that the Cluster LP (the most important tool in the state of the art approximation for Correlation Clustering) cannot be adapted in the constrained setting. 

Theorem 1.1. Under the Unique Games Conjecture, there is no (2 − ε)-factor approximation algorithm for Constrained Correlation Clustering, for any constant ε > 0. Furthermore, under the Unique Games Conjecture, there is no (2 − ε)-factor approximation algorithm for Cluster Deletion, for any constant ε > 0.

It is known that Cluster Deletion is approximable within a factor 2 [VGW18]. Our lower bound imme-diately implies that this is optimal. 

Corollary 1.2. There exists a polynomial time algorithm for Cluster Deletion whose approximation factor is (the optimal) 2.

We note that the same lower bound result was obtained independently by Cao and Xu [CX26].

A common type of analysis for such algorithms is the Triangle-Based analysis. From a high-level view, in this analysis we bound the ratio of the cost of the algorithm to the budget given by a Linear Program (LP, Figure 1), independently for every triplet of nodes (triangle). The overall approximation is upper bounded by the maximum such ratio.

• The LP relaxation in Figure 1 gives a distance in [0 , 1] for every pair of nodes, such that must-link pairs have distance 0 and cannot-link pairs have distance 1. 

• At every step of the algorithm, until all nodes are clustered, we select a supernode p (pivot) at random, with probability proportional to the number of nodes it contains. 

• For every unclustered supernode s, we decide whether to include it in the cluster of p with probability related to the LP distance of p and s, and the type of edges connecting the supernode of p and s.Crucially, if the LP distance is 0 .5 or larger, we do not cluster s together with p (hence the term strict pivoting ). Notice that hard constraints are always satisfied. This follows trivially for must-link constraints. For cannot-link constraints, notice that within a cluster every node is at distance less than 0 .5 from the pivot. Therefore, by triangle inequality, the diameter of any cluster is less than 1; as cannot-link pairs have distance exactly 1, this means that we do not violate cannot-link constraints. We show that the straightforward Triangle-Based analysis used for such algorithms cannot give a better-than- 16 7 approximation, while also presenting an algorithm that achieves 16 7 approximation. It is worth noting that for Correlation Clustering, a similar barrier exists (2 .025), but no algorithm within the corre-sponding framework achieves this bound (best known approximation is ≈ 2.06); see [CMSY15]. Similarly, for Chromatic Correlation Clustering, within the corresponding framework the lower bound 2 .11 and upper bound 2 .15 do not match [LFL25b]. We are only aware of Pseudometric-Weighted Correlation Clustering where within its corresponding framework an optimal 10 /3 approximation has been designed (however, in this case, no better algorithm is known, while for Constrained Correlation Clustering we show a 16 7 − γ

approximation).

Comparison with Correlation Clustering There is a big literature on (unconstrained) Correlation Clustering. It is therefore imperative to discuss if/how techniques that worked in the unconstrained setting can be expected to provide results for the constrained setting as well. We focus on techniques that led to better-than-3 approximations, as a 3-approximation for Constrained Correlation Clustering was already known before our work. 

• Cluster LP : The Cluster LP is an exponentially large LP that has been shown to be solvable in polynomial time [CCL +24]. It has been used to achieve the state of the art 1 .485 + ϵ approximation for Correlation Clustering. However, as shown in [KKV25], solving this LP in the constrained setting would imply a better-than-2 approximation for Constrained Correlation Clustering. As per our Theorem 1.1, this would contradict the Unique Games Conjecture.  

> 3For the knowledgeable reader, the core argument in [CLN22] shows that we cannot have too many “bad” triangles in any subgraph of 4 nodes; in our case the argument gets more technical and requires subgraphs of 6 nodes.

3• Correlated Rounding : The first algorithm achieving a 2-approximation for Correlation Clustering (which was improved to 1 .994 in the same paper [CLN22]) used a pivoting algorithm and correlated rounding. Specifically, for any two nodes u, v that differ from the pivot p, the decision on whether to include u in the cluster containing p was not independent from the decision regarding v. Unfortunately, our 16 7 barrier holds even if correlated rounding is used. Furthermore, implementing correlated round-ing that would respect the hard constraints seems to be the main barrier in solving the Constrained Cluster LP, and is thus unlikely. 

• Set-based Rounding : An important ingredient to improve the approximation from 1 .994 to 1 .73 was the set-based clustering [CLLN23]. According to the authors, this uses the “the power of [...] the correlated rounding technique”, which we already highlighted as a challenge in the constrained setting. 

• Local Search : The local search technique from [CLP +24] assumes access to some “near-optimal” clus-ters and provides a better-than-2 approximation. Even though obtaining such clusters is possible for Correlation Clustering, in the constrained setting it would automatically imply a better-than-2 approximation ([KKV25]), which would contradict the Unique Games Conjecture [Kho02]. 

• Specialized Rounding : Pairing the pivoting algorithm with specialized rounding functions has been used extensively [ACN08, CMSY15, CLN22, CLLN23, CCL +24] for Correlation Clustering. However, for Constrained Correlation Clustering, only a very simple rounding (rounding to the closest integer) has been employed [vZW09] so far. A possible reason is that the must-link constraints enforce (a special case of) correlated rounding, which turned out to be a significant challenge for our work as well. Concretely, all previous approaches use two different rounding functions, depending on whether a plus or a minus edge is rounded. In order to satisfy must-link constraints however, we need to round sets of multiple edges simultaneously, while it is not necessary for all of them to be plus (resp. minus). We show that using a convex combination of two particular functions (one for plus and one for minus edges), with the coefficients depending on the number of plus (resp. minus) edges in the set of edges we round, it suffices to consider cases where all edges are plus or all are minus 4.Perhaps surprisingly, the key property of the particular functions we use, that allows for the afore-mentioned argument, is that if a plus and a minus edge have the same LP distance, then we are more likely to cluster together the endpoints of the minus edge, rather than the endpoints of the plus edge. This was not the case, for example, in the work of [CMSY15] that introduced the specialized rounding framework. 

Summing up To summarize the contributions of our work, we identify Constrained Correlation Clustering as a variant of Correlation Clustering for which an optimal approximation might be within reach. We conjecture the optimal approximation to be 2, and prove that it lies in [2 , 16 7 ). At the same time, our results demonstrate that the Cluster LP cannot be (1 + ε) approximated in the constrained setting, and is thus unlikely to help in designing an optimal approximation. Furthermore, the optimality cannot be achieved using specialized rounding and a straightforward Triangle-Based analysis, even if the actual optimal is larger than 2; that is because we show that, unlike our upper bound, this framework cannot prove better-than- 16 7

approximations. Finally, as a byproduct of our work, we completely settle the approximability of the Cluster Deletion problem, showing that the optimal approximation factor is 2.

# 4 Algorithmic framework and 16 /7 barrier

In fact, in Section 6 we present a Strict-Pivoting algorithm that obtains a better-than- 16 7 approximation, using a more sophisticated analysis.
